What You'll Find in This Category

These communities support GATE aspirants across various engineering branches (Computer Science, Mechanical, Electrical, Civil, and others), covering subject-wise concept discussion, previous year paper analysis, PSU (Public Sector Undertaking) recruitment guidance for those using GATE scores for direct job placement, and M.Tech admission counselling for IITs and NITs.

Common Topics Discussed

Members discuss branch-specific technical concepts and problem-solving, previous year GATE paper analysis and cutoff trends, PSU recruitment processes that accept GATE scores (like ONGC, NTPC, and others), M.Tech admission counselling through COAP, and general exam strategy for GATE's unique mix of technical and general aptitude questions.

Why GATE Aspirants Join These Groups

GATE serves the dual purpose of M.Tech admission and, increasingly, direct PSU recruitment, making it valuable for both fresh graduates and working engineers considering a career shift. Branch-specific peer discussion helps clarify technical concepts that may need revisiting since graduation, and PSU-focused members value shared updates on which organizations are currently recruiting through GATE scores.

Tips Before You Join

Join branch-specific groups matching your exact engineering discipline, since GATE papers differ significantly by branch and generic engineering groups may not offer relevant technical discussion. For PSU recruitment discussion, verify current recruitment notifications directly through each PSU's official careers page, since GATE-based recruitment cycles change year to year.

GATE's Dual Purpose: Higher Studies and PSU Jobs

Beyond M.Tech admission at IITs and NITs (processed through COAP - Common Offer Acceptance Portal), a growing number of PSUs use GATE scores directly for recruitment, skipping separate written exams. This dual pathway means GATE aspirants often fall into two overlapping groups — those purely seeking higher education and those targeting stable government-sector engineering jobs — and many groups cater to both audiences with relevant, branch-specific discussion.
